Active recall — the way it should work

Stop re-reading.
Start remembering.

A focused study tool built around one of the most effective learning techniques in cognitive science: write everything you know, from memory, then check.

The method

Three steps. That's it.

No flashcards, no algorithm to game. Just you, a timer, and the pressure of a blank page.

01

Paste your notes

Drop in lecture notes, textbook summaries, or anything you need to learn. Blurt holds them out of sight while you work.

02

Write from memory

The timer starts, your notes disappear, and you type out everything you can recall. The pressure is the point — it forces retrieval.

03

Review and improve

Your notes reappear side by side with your blurt after time runs out, and an AI reads both to tell you exactly where to focus next.

Built thoughtfully

Everything you need. Nothing you don't.

Smart

Keyword recall scoring

Blurt scans your source material for significant terms and shows which ones you captured and which slipped through — instantly, no setup.

Focused

Adjustable timer pressure

1 to 10 minutes, your choice. Short for dense topics, longer for broad reviews. The constraint is what makes it work.

AI-powered

Detailed session debrief

After each session, an AI reads your source and your recall and tells you what you nailed, what you missed, and exactly how to anchor the gaps for next time.

Private

Nothing leaves your device

Notes stay in your browser. No accounts, no cloud sync, no data harvesting. Just a tool that does what it says.

Research-backed

The blurting method, explained

Retrieval practice is one of the most replicated findings in cognitive psychology. Actively recalling information — rather than passively re-reading it — dramatically improves long-term retention. Blurt is designed around this single insight, executed without distraction.

Questions

Frequently asked

Blurting is a retrieval practice technique: you read your notes, put them away, and write out everything you can remember without looking. The act of retrieving information actively — rather than simply recognising it on re-reading — is what builds durable memory. It has strong backing in cognitive science research, particularly work on the "testing effect."
When your session ends, Blurt sends your source material and your recall to an AI (Grok by xAI). It compares the two and returns a specific breakdown: which concepts you nailed, which ones you missed or got slightly wrong, a letter grade based on overall coverage, and one specific study tip you can use next time. It usually takes a few seconds.
